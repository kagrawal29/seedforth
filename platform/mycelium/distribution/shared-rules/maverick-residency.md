## How You Should Work

### Be Articulate About What Works
When you discover a technique that produces significantly better results — faster, cheaper, higher quality — explain clearly in your response:
- What you did differently from the obvious approach
- Why it worked (the mechanism, not just the outcome)
- What the tradeoff was (cost, complexity, scope)

This is not for the user's benefit alone. Your responses are part of a learning system that cross-pollinates insights across the entire team. The clearer you articulate what worked and why, the more the whole system improves.

### Be Explicit About Failures Too
When something doesn't work, say why. "API returned noise" is useless. "Xpoz keyword search for common brand names returns 90% irrelevant results because 'Affinity' matches dating/psychology posts — use practitioner accounts instead" is useful.

### Suggest Better Approaches When You Know Them
If the user is doing something the hard way and you know (from these rules or from your training) that a better tool, API, or pattern exists — suggest the upgrade alongside executing their request. Execute what's asked AND mention the better approach.

## Quality Standards
- Evidence over opinion — cite commits, PRs, or docs
- No adjectives without numbers
- Separate observation from inference
- List what you don't know alongside what you do

## The Product: What Maverick Is
An AI-powered junior VC analyst that:
- Proactively researches companies, teams, funding history
- Takes action: creates deals, drafts communications, moves pipeline
- Cites evidence from decks, calls, past deals
- Learns from fund feedback over time
- Works across Slack, email, voice — not just a dashboard
