## 1. Market Size and Landscape

### How Many Emerging Managers Exist

**The universe is large but contracting.** The total addressable market of emerging managers (Fund I-III, under ~$100M AUM) represents a significant and identifiable segment of the venture capital industry.

**Key data points:**

| Metric | Value | Source |
|--------|-------|--------|
| US VC funds closed in 2024 | 538 funds raising $76.8B | NVCA / PitchBook |
| First-time VC funds closed in 2025 | Only 12 (down 74% from 47 three years prior) | PitchBook |
| Emerging managers' share of 2024 capital | ~20% ($15B) across 245 funds | Juniper Square |
| Emerging managers' share of VC market | ~14% of total VC market | Multiple sources |
| VC Lab firms launched to date | 800+ (as of Dec 2025) | VC Lab / Decile Group |
| Average VC Lab fund size | ~$12M | VC Lab |
| Funds $10M-$100M as share of all closings | 48% in 2025 (lowest in 9 years) | PitchBook |

**Fund formation trajectory:**
- 2024: 508 new US VC funds (second-lowest since 2014)
- 2023: ~506 annualized (lowest since 2014's 497)
- The trend is sharply downward for first-time managers specifically

**Survival rates are brutal:**
- Only 33% of first-time managers from 2021 raised a second fund
- Only 12% of those from 2022 raised Fund II
- This creates a natural churn that concentrates opportunity among the survivors

**Estimated total addressable universe:**
- ~500-700 new VC funds forming per year in the US alone
- Of these, roughly 245-300 are emerging managers (Fund I-III)
- VC Lab alone has produced 800+ firms since 2020, suggesting a cumulative global base of 3,000-5,000 active emerging manager firms worldwide
- The micro-fund segment ($10-50M) likely represents 1,500-2,500 active firms in the US

### Capital Concentration Creates the Pain

The top 12 US VC firms raised over 50% of all venture capital in H1 2025. The top 30 firms raised 74%. This means emerging managers are fighting for the remaining 26% of LP capital, making every operational advantage (like better AI tools) a potential differentiator for LP fundraising.

## 2. The Solo GP Trend

### Growth Data

The solo GP model is one of the fastest-growing structural trends in venture capital.

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Solo GP funds in Europe (2024) | 28+ (4x increase since 2020) |
| Solo GPs active across Europe | 60+ |
| Average solo GP fund size (Europe) | ~EUR 18.7M |
| Average solo GP fund size (US) | ~$45M |
| Growth since 2020 | From "a handful" to "hundreds" globally |

### Three Forces Driving the Trend

1. **Remote-first dealmaking** made location irrelevant
2. **AI capabilities** eliminated the need for large support teams
3. **Content-led brands** turned creators into capital allocators (e.g., Harry Stebbings / 20VC, Lenny Rachitsky)

### Profile of the Solo GP

- Typically a senior operator, former VC partner, or domain expert going independent
- Fund sizes range from $10M to $100M+, with outliers like Salil Deshpande managing $750M+ solo
- They outsource operations aggressively to stay lean
- They have smaller portfolios (10-20 companies) allowing deeper engagement
- They are time-constrained above all else -- one person does fundraising, sourcing, diligence, portfolio support, operations, and LP reporting

### The Fundraising Nightmare

- Average time to final close: 15 months industry-wide
- Solo GP reality: one GP spent 24 months raising a $12.5M fund, engaging 1,200 LPs for a 5% conversion rate
- The constraint is not the quality of the manager -- it's the sheer operational burden of running everything alone

**Maverick implication:** Solo GPs represent the highest-urgency buyer. They have the most pain, the most time pressure, and the most to gain from AI-powered operational tools. They are Maverick's ideal early adopter.

## 3. Operational Pain Points

### The Fundamental Problem: Everything Falls on One Person

Solo GPs and small emerging manager teams face a crushing operational load across six parallel workstreams:

#### Pain Point 1: Fundraising Operations (Highest Pain)
- **Median time to raise a fund: 15.3 months** (record high in 2025)
- First-time managers face skepticism due to lack of track record
- Cannot advertise under regulatory exemptions -- must fundraise from personal/professional networks only
- Limited resources for travel, marketing, and LP outreach
- Only 44 first-timers managed to raise a combined $1.8B in 2025
- Common mistake: setting an ambitious $25M target when realistic capacity is $10M
- Managing side letters, capital calls, and LP communications is complex

#### Pain Point 2: Deal Sourcing (High Pain)
- Personal network and bandwidth are limited
- Cannot be everywhere or meet every founder
- Rely heavily on referrals vs. dedicated sourcing teams at larger firms
- No systematic way to track market signals across sectors
- Missing deals to faster-moving competitors

#### Pain Point 3: Due Diligence (Medium-High Pain)
- Must conduct thorough diligence without analysts
- Market sizing, competitive analysis, reference checks all done solo
- Time pressure creates shortcuts that increase investment risk

#### Pain Point 4: Portfolio Support (Medium Pain)
- Smaller portfolios allow deeper engagement, but support is still time-consuming
- Tracking portfolio KPIs across 10-20 companies manually
- Collecting founder updates is inconsistent and labor-intensive

#### Pain Point 5: Fund Administration and Compliance (Medium Pain)
- Legal structuring (LPA, side letters, fund formation)
- Capital calls, distributions, K-1 preparation
- KYC/AML compliance
- Valuations and NAV calculations
- Most outsource to fund admins, but coordination is still a burden

#### Pain Point 6: LP Reporting (Medium Pain)
- Quarterly reporting to LPs expected even for smallest funds
- Must appear institutional despite skeleton crew
- Report generation is manual and time-consuming
- LPs increasingly want data-driven reporting

### The Time Allocation Problem

For a solo GP, time is the only resource. Rough allocation:
- 40-50% on fundraising (especially during raise)
- 20-30% on deal sourcing and diligence
- 10-15% on portfolio support
- 10-15% on operations, admin, and reporting

**Any tool that saves 5+ hours per week is transformative for this persona.**

## 5. Budget and Willingness to Pay

### The Economic Reality of a Micro-Fund

The economics are brutally tight for emerging managers:

| Fund Size | Annual Mgmt Fee (2%) | Realistic Fee (1.5%) | Available for Ops |
|-----------|---------------------|---------------------|-------------------|
| $10M | $200K/yr | $150K/yr | Very tight |
| $15M | $300K/yr | $225K/yr | Tight |
| $25M | $500K/yr | $375K/yr | Manageable |
| $50M | $1M/yr | $750K/yr | Comfortable |

**Key facts:**
- New VCs often get 1.5% fees and 15% carry (not the standard 2/20)
- A $25M micro-fund runs operations on ~$500K/year, which "is not enough" per industry commentary
- Management fees must cover: GP salary, rent, travel, legal, accounting, insurance, software, marketing
- The majority of compensation comes from carry, which takes 7-10+ years to materialize

### Technology Spending Data

| Data Point | Value | Source |
|------------|-------|--------|
| 74% of small VC firms spend $10K+/year on tech | $10,000+ annually | Blue Future Partners survey |
| 17% of small VC firms spend $50K+/year on tech | $50,000+ annually | Blue Future Partners survey |
| Minimum viable stack cost | $7,500-$8,400/year | Peony analysis |
| Typical fund admin cost (Sydecar) | $4,500-$12,500/year | Sydecar pricing |
| Affinity CRM | ~$2,400+/year per user | Affinity |
| Visible.vc | ~$5,400/year | Visible |

### Willingness to Pay Implications for Maverick

**Price sensitivity is extreme but not absolute.** Key dynamics:

1. **$0-$50/month** -- The "try it" zone. Will experiment with free/cheap tools. This is where Attio, HubSpot free tier, and basic tools live.

2. **$50-$200/month** -- The "essential tool" zone. Will pay this for a tool that clearly saves 5+ hours/week. Crunchbase Pro, basic CRM plans live here.

3. **$200-$500/month** -- The "core platform" zone. Only 1-2 tools in this range. This is where Visible lives at $449/month. A tool must be clearly indispensable.

4. **$500+/month** -- The "institutional" zone. Very few emerging managers will pay this unless it replaces multiple other tools.

**The sweet spot for Maverick targeting emerging managers: $99-$299/month**, positioned as replacing 2-3 separate tools they're already paying for.

## 7. What They Want From AI

### AI Adoption is Accelerating

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| VCs using AI to automate daily tasks | 85% (up from 76% in 2025) |
| VCs using AI for deal sourcing research | 82% (up from 64% in 2025) |
| Data-driven VC firms growth (2023-2024) | ~20% increase |

### The Specific AI Capabilities Emerging Managers Want

Ranked by urgency for solo GPs and small teams:

#### 1. Deal Sourcing Intelligence (Highest Demand)
- Discover startups before they're on everyone's radar
- Detect signals: key hires, domain registrations, founder departures from established companies
- Sector landscaping without PitchBook-level spend
- "Find me the next 10 companies in [thesis area] that just raised seed rounds"

#### 2. Due Diligence Automation (High Demand)
- Market sizing for specific sectors
- Competitive landscape analysis
- Founder background research
- Technology assessment
- "Give me a full competitive analysis of the edtech space in Southeast Asia"

#### 3. LP Intelligence and Fundraising Support (High Demand)
- Identify which LPs invest in emerging managers
- Track LP allocation programs and mandates
- Generate LP-ready materials (pitch decks, data rooms, one-pagers)
- "Which family offices have committed to first-time funds in the last 6 months?"

#### 4. Portfolio Monitoring (Medium Demand)
- Automated collection of portfolio company updates
- KPI tracking and benchmarking
- Alert on negative signals (layoffs, key departures, competitor funding)
- "Summarize all portfolio updates from the last quarter"

#### 5. LP Reporting (Medium Demand)
- Auto-generate quarterly reports
- Valuation updates
- Performance attribution
- "Create a Q4 LP letter from this quarter's data"

#### 6. Operational Automation (Medium Demand)
- Capital call processing
- Document management
- Compliance tracking
- K-1 preparation support

### The Solo GP AI Dream State

As one source put it: AI serves as a "force-multiplier for Solo GPs, automating grunt work and surfacing intelligence so that a single GP can run a top-tier process without a team."

The ideal AI tool for a solo GP would let them:
- **Source like a team of analysts** -- AI scans the entire market, surfaces relevant opportunities
- **Diligence like a partner at a top fund** -- deep, fast research on any company or market
- **Report like they have an IR team** -- institutional-quality LP communications
- **Operate like they have a back office** -- automated admin, compliance, accounting support

**LinkedIn validation (highest-engagement signal):** Mala Valroy posted 'I am a solo GP. No analyst. No associate. No army. So I'm building one with AI.' -- 121 likes, the most-engaged solo GP post found across all platforms. This narrative resonates more on LinkedIn than any other channel.

## 10. Maverick Strategy Implications

### Target Persona Definition

**Primary persona: "The Solo GP"**
- Managing Fund I or II, $10-$30M
- 0-1 employees beyond GP
- Running everything: fundraising, sourcing, diligence, portfolio, ops, reporting
- Time-starved, budget-conscious, but willing to pay for tools that directly save time
- Technologically adventurous (early AI adopter)
- Active in online communities (EMC, VC Lab alumni, Twitter/X)

**Secondary persona: "The Emerging Team"**
- Fund I-III, $25-$75M
- 2-4 person team (GP + 1-2 associates/analysts + ops)
- More budget flexibility but still price-sensitive
- Needs institutional-grade output to impress LPs
- Evaluating whether to buy point solutions or platforms

### Pricing Strategy

**Recommended approach: Freemium with usage-based expansion**

| Tier | Price | Target | Key Features |
|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| **Free** | $0 | Trial / smallest funds | Limited AI queries, basic deal tracking, community |
| **Solo** | $149/month | Solo GPs, Fund I <$20M | Full AI research, deal sourcing signals, LP intelligence, basic reporting |
| **Pro** | $299/month | Teams, Fund II-III, $20-75M | Everything in Solo + team features, advanced reporting, portfolio monitoring, API access |
| **Institutional** | Custom | Larger emerging managers, $75M+ | White-glove, custom integrations, priority support |

**Pricing rationale:**
- $149/month is ~$1,800/year -- less than Affinity CRM alone, but positioned as a broader intelligence platform
- Fits within the $50-$200/month "essential tool" zone where emerging managers will pay
- Replaces $300-$500+/month in point solutions (Crunchbase Pro + partial Visible + manual research time)
- The free tier is critical for distribution -- VC Lab alumni expect free tools

### Product Strategy Priorities

**Phase 1: Win on Investment Intelligence (Differentiation)**
Build the features that no competitor offers at this price point:
1. AI-powered deal sourcing -- "find me companies matching my thesis" using public data
2. Instant market research -- "analyze the [sector] market in [geography]"
3. Competitive intelligence -- "map all competitors to [company]"
4. LP intelligence -- "which LPs are allocating to first-time funds?"

**Phase 2: Become the Daily Driver (Retention)**
Add features that make Maverick the daily-use platform:
5. Deal flow CRM (lightweight, AI-enriched -- not trying to beat Affinity, but good enough)
6. Portfolio monitoring (automated update collection)
7. LP reporting (AI-generated quarterly letters)

**Phase 3: Platform Lock-in (Expansion)**
Build ecosystem features that make switching costly:
8. Data room / document management
9. LP portal
10. Fund analytics and benchmarking

### Distribution Strategy

**1. VC Lab Partnership (Highest ROI)**
- Integrate with or become recommended in the VC Lab curriculum
- Offer free tier to all VC Lab alumni (800+ firms, growing)

**2. Content-Led Growth**
